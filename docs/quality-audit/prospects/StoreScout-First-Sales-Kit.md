## The buyer to test first

Start with an independent Shopify CRO/growth consultant or boutique email/CRO agency where one person can approve a $29 tool. Qualify the workflow rather than guessing company size or revenue:

1. They prepare competitor research for an active Shopify client at least monthly.
2. They can name two or three relevant competitors and a decision or client meeting in the next two weeks.
3. Those stores have accessible, comparable public catalogs in a confirmed currency/market.
4. The buyer wants pricing, markdown, assortment, or catalog-change evidence; they do not require sales estimates, Amazon monitoring, automatic repricing, or regional price guarantees.
5. They can explain what they currently do and what is frustrating about it.

The secondary segment is a hands-on Shopify brand operator planning an actual promotion. Focus on one category at a time, such as apparel or beauty, so examples are comparable. Revenue is not inferable from the storefront; do not label businesses “high revenue” without evidence.

Agencies are a hypothesis, not a proven best segment. If five relevant agency conversations show no recurring need but operators describe a concrete buying trigger, shift the next batch toward operators. Do not spend the two weeks trying to persuade people who do not do this job.

## The offer and the paid ask

### Primary: assisted Pro onboarding

**Price:** $29/month, monthly billing. This is the Pro price in the reviewed frontend, not a newly invented discount. The source lists ten competitors, daily scans, and 90-day history; verify production entitlements before promising those features. [Current plan definitions](https://github.com/Devonwaldrop01/StoreScout/blob/e66e3f05417cea5d57f27800c755c2353ca2b791/frontend/app/page.tsx).

**Initial delivery:** personally configure three supported competitors, explain coverage, and provide one checked brief for the customer's next client review or promotion decision. Agree a delivery date before payment. Cap the initial assisted cohort at three only if that matches your actual available support time. Do not create fake scarcity.

**Free sample:** one competitor, a few source-backed observations, and the scan date. Keep preparation to about ten minutes. The sample should make it possible to judge the paid work; it should not become an unpaid custom strategy project.

**Paid brief:** one page containing the three competitors, observation date and market/currency, catalog coverage, three verifiable findings with source links, and one proposed question or experiment. Distinguish observations from interpretation. Do not promise an increase in revenue. On the first observation you have a snapshot, not evidence of a historical change; only use “changed” when there is a comparable earlier observation.

**Close:** “Would you use this in your upcoming client review? Pro is $29/month. I can help set up your three competitors and prepare the first checked brief. If that is useful, I’ll send the checkout link and we’ll agree the delivery date.”

Verify the supported stores before sending checkout. Keep cancellation, recurring billing, and the scope of personal help clear. Offer the $79 Agency tier only when a customer's verified needs exceed Pro and the necessary Agency features work. Do not lead with annual prepayment, lifetime deals, or a new pricing system.

### Contingency: a manual research service

If the software cannot pass the readiness gates promptly, offer **“StoreScout competitor brief — $29 one time.”** Research three agreed stores manually, confirm that enough public information is available before charging, deliver one dated brief within an agreed window, and state that ongoing monitoring is not included. Use an existing functioning payment or invoice route with clear fulfillment terms. No payment link was created in this review.

Suggested message:

> I’m offering a one-time competitor brief for Shopify client work: three agreed stores, public pricing and promotion observations, source links, and one question to take into your next review. It’s $29 once, with no subscription. I’ll confirm the stores are suitable before you pay and agree a delivery date. Want to see a short sample for one competitor?

A sale here validates willingness to pay for the research service. It does not establish demand for the software subscription or retention. Do not combine these two outcomes in your metrics.

### Post 3 — LinkedIn educational example; publish after the calculation fix

A $10 variant and a $100 variant do not automatically make a 90% discount.

Suppose the $10 option has no compare-at price. The $100 option has a compare-at price of $100. Neither is marked down.

Taking the cheapest selling price and comparing it with a different variant's compare-at price invents a promotion that does not exist.

When checking a Shopify competitor, compare the same variant, confirm the currency, and keep the source link. Then ask whether the observation should affect your client's next decision.

That is the standard I’m working toward with StoreScout. If you prepare competitor briefs for Shopify clients, message me a store you find difficult to compare. I’ll check whether it fits the tool and show you the evidence.

**Use:** demonstrate the quality of the thinking. This is an explicitly hypothetical example, not a claim about a real merchant. **Success signal:** relevant examples or sample requests from practitioners.

## Scorecard and decision rules

These are **planning targets, not predicted conversion rates**: 40 qualified first contacts, eight positive replies, five useful sample reviews/demos, and one to three paid, activated customers. The eight named prospects are the researched starting list; additional qualification work is explicitly included in the schedule.

Count one person once at each funnel stage. A positive reply describes a relevant problem or agrees to evaluate a sample; a polite “interesting” does not qualify. A paid SaaS activation means an independent buyer paid the stated price and obtained a usable result. Record refunds and cancellations separately. A service sale belongs in the service column.

Use a simple log: **prospect → fit source → buyer/workflow → contact route → message/date → reply → sample → paid ask → outcome → next action/date**. Initially every researched prospect's status is “uncontacted.” Keep actual responses in their own words.

- **After 20 qualified contacts plus the scheduled follow-up:** fewer than two concrete acknowledgements of the problem means revise the segment or opening question before sending the next batch. Do not immediately add features.
- **After five relevant sample reviews with no payment:** ask what made the output insufficient, what alternative they use, and whether the decision recurs. Distinguish poor data, wrong workflow, lack of urgency, and price objections.
- **After a payment with no useful result:** repair fulfillment before acquiring more customers.
- **After useful first delivery but no later use:** you have evidence of initial value, not retention. Interview the buyer before scaling.

Ask five discovery questions: “When did you last do this research?” “What decision was it for?” “How did you do it?” “What was difficult or unreliable?” “If this sample solved that problem, would you pay $29 to use it for the next review?” Avoid relying on “Would you use an app like this?” as validation.

## What the first sale would mean for your larger goal

One full-price sale from someone outside your personal circle would be a useful first signal. A second use and a renewal would be stronger. It is not yet evidence that StoreScout can replace a salary.

At the $29 Pro price, ten customers represent $290/month, fifty $1,450/month, and one hundred $2,900/month in gross subscription revenue before payment fees, hosting, AI, support, taxes, and churn. Those are arithmetic scenarios, not forecasts. Track real costs and retained customers before planning around the income.

For these two weeks, keep the question small enough to answer: **will a real Shopify practitioner pay for a competitor brief, use it in current work, and want the next one?** The repository already contains enough product surface to test that once the trust blockers are resolved. More features are not the next proof.
